# B1 Preliminary reference for this project

This guide is a working summary of Cambridge English's public B1 Preliminary format. Check the linked Cambridge pages again when revising the authoring rules; exam specifications and vocabulary lists can change.

## Current component shape

The exam has four equally weighted components, each 25%: Reading (45 minutes, 6 parts, 32 questions), Writing (45 minutes, 2 parts, 2 answers), Listening (about 30 minutes including 6 minutes to transfer answers, 4 parts, 25 questions), and Speaking (about 12 minutes for a pair, 4 parts). Reading, Writing, and Listening formats are shared between B1 Preliminary and B1 Preliminary for Schools.

### Reading: 6 parts, 32 one-mark questions

1. Five short real-world notices/messages; 5 multiple-choice items testing the main message.
2. Match 5 people/descriptions to 8 short texts on one topic; detailed matching.
3. One longer text; 5 multiple-choice items on detail, gist, inference, global meaning, attitude/opinion.
4. One longer text with 5 removed sentences; choose from 6 options to restore cohesion and structure.
5. Short text with 6 multiple-choice lexical gaps; test vocabulary in context.
6. Short text with 6 open gaps; one word per gap, often grammar/function words and common lexis.

### Writing: 2 answers, about 100 words each

1. Compulsory email replying to a message and addressing four linked notes/content points.
2. Candidate chooses one of two tasks: an article or a story. Give audience/purpose for an article; provide the exact opening sentence for a story.

Cambridge assesses each writing response on four subscales: Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, and Language, each 0–5. These are human rating criteria; the app may provide coaching prompts but must not represent heuristic feedback as an official score.

### Listening: 4 parts, 25 one-mark questions; each recording heard twice

1. Seven short monologues/dialogues; identify key information, conventionally with visual choices.
2. Six short dialogues; multiple choice on gist.
3. One monologue; complete six notes/gaps, one word per gap.
4. One longer interview; six multiple-choice questions on detail, meaning, attitude/opinion.

### Speaking: 4 parts

1. Personal interview with the interlocutor on familiar topics.
2. Each candidate speaks for about one minute describing a photograph. Prepare one original visual brief and elicit people, place, actions and reasonable speculation.
3. Candidates discuss a situation together using visual prompts and reach a decision. Provide one clear scenario, at least four related image briefs, and a decision question. This app adapts the paired activity for solo rehearsal by recording one answer per visual; it does not replace paired interaction.
4. Follow-up discussion with the interlocutor on the Part 3 topic. Provide open questions that invite reasons, examples and comparisons.

Speaking is not objectively marked by this app. Recordings and browser-generated transcripts are practice aids only. Illustrations are original prompts, not official Cambridge photographs.

## Vocabulary and language targets

Use the official vocabulary list as a broad guide, not an exhaustive whitelist. Spread topics across exams and include functional language and high-frequency grammar in natural contexts: present/past/future forms, present perfect, comparatives/superlatives, modals, questions, countable/uncountable nouns, articles, prepositions, conjunctions, basic conditionals, infinitive/gerund patterns, common phrasal verbs, and cohesive devices. Keep the task focused on comprehension or communication rather than isolated grammar testing unless the task type specifically assesses lexical/grammatical gap filling.

Rotate lexical fields across everyday domains: personal life, relationships, education, work, travel and transport, places/services, food, health, shopping, hobbies, media, technology, nature/weather, and events. Include British English as the default voice and spelling while allowing other English accents in listening; keep spelling variety consistent within a writing task.

## Quality checklist

- Counts and types match the part specification exactly.
- All questions have one defensible key and keys point to valid options.
- Every text/script is original, coherent, and plausibly B1; names, dates, prices, places, and logistics remain consistent.
- Questions follow the order of information when the task convention expects it.
- Distractors reflect likely misunderstandings and paraphrase; they do not rely on outside knowledge.
- Reading gap-text choices test reference, linking, chronology, or cohesion; the extra option is genuinely plausible but does not fit.
- Open cloze answers are one word each and grammatical in context; record acceptable variants only when they are truly equivalent.
- Listening scripts can be spoken clearly by browser speech synthesis. Keep clip boundaries explicit, avoid confusing speaker labels, and ensure question paraphrases do not simply reveal the answer.
- Speaking prompts should elicit connected B1 speech: familiar present, past and future topics, reasons, examples, preferences, comparisons and simple speculation. Ground photo descriptions in visible details and make Part 3 options comparable so candidates can discuss and decide.
- Writing prompts specify audience, purpose, content points, and approximate length. Story opening sentence is preserved exactly in the answer instructions.
- Topic and vocabulary coverage is not duplicated mechanically across the existing catalogue.
